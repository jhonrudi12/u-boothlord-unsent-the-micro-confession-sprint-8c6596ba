A two-part micro-confession — a To: line and the one line that was typed and never sent — written by a deterministic Python program for make.fun's "Unsent: The Micro-Confession Sprint" bounty:

> To: Dad
>
> I typed that I quit the job you still brag about to your friends, then deleted it and told you the promotion went through.

It is not a joke, a greeting, or advice. It is a confession of a lie told instead of the truth: the job is gone, the bragging goes on, and the promotion that "went through" never happened. The reason it stayed in drafts is felt, not stated — the fear of disappointing the one person it was written to. Swap the addressee and the confession breaks: only a father brags about his child's job to his friends like that.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the confession (130 characters, two lines) to `/output/entry.txt`.
